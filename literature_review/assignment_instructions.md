1.         Summary
Perform a literature survey on the machine learning or data mining topics related to your research project. The goal of the survey is to help you better understand the research problem you are working on and give you an opportunity to get new ideas on how to address your project but also support you in making informed decisions on the methodology you will end up using for your final results. For this task, you need to do some in-depth reading of relevant resources on the internet and/or in the UoA library. This will involve finding articles that describe a problem and/or approaches to solving a problem. You should prefer published peer-reviewed papers and book chapters over blogs and non- peer-reviewed manuscripts where possible. You should map the current problems in the area you are working on; what approaches have already been used and what their strengths and weaknesses are. Given several different approaches, you may be able to organize them along several dimensions, which can give your insight into what possible approaches can be developed that have not yet been investigated. The weaknesses are especially important as these should give you suggestions about where you might be able to contribute. Regarding the latter, pay attention to the related work section in the papers, and sections that could be called "Discussion", "Conclusions" and "Future Work".

We know parts of the survey will be written by different students, but one student can take the role of lead author and smooth differences and guide the other group members in the writing process so that the text formatting and notation use is consistent, no text duplication happens (due to reintroduction of concepts or equations in the parts contributed by different students), the logical structure and the overall story is maintained.

Note that most of the peer-reviewed high-quality research published work is also written by multiple authors, but the papers always read as they were written by a single author. Think of the reader when you write; that reader could be someone like you who is starting in the research field  and needs clarity and guidance more than the experts; but it could also be someone more experienced who you need to convince that you have done your homework and have thoroughly checked the facts and evidence before jumping to conclusions.

Appendix A. Tips on how to structure your literature survey
Your literature survey should follow the structure of a research paper in order to prepare you for the final report. That means it consists of Title, Authors, Abstract, Introduction, Body, Conclusion, and References. Looking at many different research papers helps you to understand the structure better, but we also provide you here with some hints. Note that the structure alone will not guarantee you a certain grade.

Abstract (1 paragraph, about 150-200 words): Briefly summarize the scope, methodology and findings in the paper.
Introduction (about 1-1.5 pages): Explain the problem and why it is important. The purpose of the Introduction is to introduce the problem and the big picture to the reader and place the new proposed research/results within the relevant research and application domain. If there’s a specific research question that you are interested in or have a hypothesis, state it here. Finally, clearly state what the main contributions of your work are and how the rest of the survey is organized.
Body: Give an organized overview of the important research that has been done in the field of interest. Make it clear how and why you chose the papers that you included. Organize the papers such that the reader gets a good overview of the field. Highlight differences/similarities as well as advantages/disadvantages between the approaches.
To supplement the textual description, you could give a high-level table summary of the papers covered. A good example of a literature survey are the following papers:
A survey on concept drift adaptation (Links to an external site.),Links to an external site. Bifet, A., Bouchachia, A., Gama, J., Pechenizkiy, M., & Zliobaite, I. ACM Comput. Surv., 2014.
A Review on Multi-Label Learning Algorithms (Links to an external ),Links to an external site. by Zhang, M., &Zhou, Z. (2014). IEEE TKDE.
Note that both papers are much longer than what we expect (and typically prepared over several months); they are given to you as an example on how to approach the survey task.
Notation has to be introduced and consistently used. Relevant equations should be typed in the document (do not take screen shots from other papers) – you could do that in MS Word or LaTeX. Images can be adapted or copied if properly cited in the captions and listed in the Reference section. Quotes should be used sparingly. Try to rephrase the text you have read in other papers.
Conclusion (about 0.5 pages, maximum 1 page): Briefly summarize your findings as well as research gaps that you have identified. Clearly state what research question or hypothesis you will work on in the following weeks.
Author Contribution This section is optional, but it is a good practice of transparency when it comes to how each author contributed to the writing and design of the undertaken research work. Many serious conferences and journals include this. You can include one sentence for each group member and its contributions: Example: X. led the conceptual design of the survey wrote the Abstract, Introduction and Conclusion, re-wrote part Z in the Section Y, read references R1-R3, and proofread and made final changes in the final survey. This will be helpful for the teaching team when we will combine this information with the peer-reviews that you will submit after the survey is submitted.
References List of all cited work in the survey (use consistent IEEE reference format, check the template above).
