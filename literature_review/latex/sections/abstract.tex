\begin{abstract}
Regional English accent can affect automatic speech recognition (ASR)
performance, but its effect on final speech-translation quality is still
unclear. This survey examines how accent may affect two speech translation
approaches: \textbf{Direct speech translation}, which maps speech directly to
translated text, and \textbf{Cascaded speech translation}, which first applies
ASR and then machine translation. The survey reviews 27 studies covering
speech-translation architectures, accent robustness, ASR error propagation,
datasets, evaluation metrics, and statistical comparison. Existing research
shows that neither Direct nor Cascaded systems are consistently superior;
performance varies with language pair, training resources, test data,
reference translations, and evaluation metrics. Accent-related ASR
differences are well established, and recognition errors can reduce downstream
translation quality, but these findings do not directly show that regional
accent affects final translation quality. Common Voice and CoVoST 2 provide
suitable data for this evaluation, although accent labels, repeated content,
speaker dependence, and reference design introduce limitations. The main
research gap is therefore a matched comparison of Direct and Cascaded systems
using the same regional-English utterances and final translation quality as
the shared outcome.
\end{abstract}
