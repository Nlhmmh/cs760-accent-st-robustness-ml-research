Structure of the Final Report
Your report should follow the structure of a standard scientific research paper published in the area of Computer Science/Machine Learning. That means it consists of Title, Authors, Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, Methodology, Results, Conclusion, (Contributions) and References.

Looking at many different research papers helps you to understand the structure better, but we also provide you here with some hints. Note that the structure alone will not guarantee you a certain grade.


Abstract (1 paragraph, about 150-200 words): Briefly summarize the scope, methodology and findings of your research work.
Introduction (about 1-1.5 pages): Explain the problem and why it is important. The purpose of the Introduction is to put the reader in the picture and place the research/experiment within a context. Clearly state what is the research question you are trying to answer? This can be an experimental question, “will algorithm X perform better than algorithm Y on this dataset?” Or it can be about a new algorithm ‘can we develop an algorithm based on “otters”, Otter Colony Optimization?’ Or it can even be theoretical, “can we prove when Ant Colony Optimization will find a global optimum?”. Then you need to briefly explain how you will answer the research questions, including the hypothesis that you will test. Introduction typically concludes with main contribution of the work you did.
Related Work (1 page maximum!!!): Brief organized description of previous research related to your problem at hand. Please do not copy just whole section from your literature You need to focus only the relevant bits and it must be clear how the methodology and work you have now competed sits in the context of the relevant literature.
Methodology: Description of the methodology used to test your hypothesis or description of the case study used to demonstrate the validity of the hypothesis. We must understand how you went about testing your If it was experimental, did you use a good experimental methodology and appropriate statistics to support your results?
Results: Description of final For most of you this will include results of experiments and statistics supporting them.
Conclusion (about 1 page): Brief high-level summary of the work presented and description of future Discuss here (or in a separate section before the conclusion) which limitations you identified in your approach. If you were going to continue this work as a master’s project, what further avenues should be explored?
Contributions You should include one-two sentences for each group member and its contributions in the execution of the project and writing of the So, be specific not only about what you wrote, but also about coding, experiments, data analysis and data visualization you did. This will be helpful for the teaching team when we will combine this information with the peer-reviews that you will submit after the report is submitted.
References (minimum 9 references, maximum 1 page in IEEE format, as you did already for your Literature Survey).
Please use IEEE standard (two column) paper format for your report (https://www.ieee.org/conferences/publishing/templates.htmlLinks to an external site.). Your report should be not more than 12 pages long, excluding the References and Contributions sections. We do not allow an appendix, so please make the paper self-contained (that is, all the information a reader might need can be found in these 12 pages).

 

*Important note regarding Methodology and Results

Methodology and Results build the body of your work and depend on the topic you choose. If you propose a new technique, you will want to split the remaining pages more or less 50/50 or 40/60 into “Proposed Method” (here you describe your proposed method) and “Experiments” sections (here you describe how you run experiments, point to your implementation, include plots or statistics and discuss the results). If you do not propose a novel technique, you’ll want to put more emphasis on the description of how you tested your hypothesis. That’s what you do in a “Methodology” section before you report and discuss the results in the “Results” section.

Whichever you choose, make sure that you explain clearly why you made the decisions you made and give enough details such that the reader could reproduce everything you did based on the paper only (but at the same time avoid overwhelming the reader with implementation details; be clear and concise as possible).

All useful guidelines for the survey can be applied in writing the final report as well. The writing the report is a group work task, the result of collaborative effort/writing of the group members. It has to read as one coherent piece of text. Notation must be introduced and consistently used. Relevant equations should be typed in the document (do not take screen shots from other papers).

Images can be adapted or copied if properly cited in the captions and listed in the Reference section. Quotes should be used sparingly. Try to rephrase the text you have read in other papers.
