\begin{abstract}
Regional English accents may affect speech recognition, but their association
with final translation quality requires separate evaluation. This study compares
one Direct and two Cascaded English-to-Simplified-Chinese systems on 4,200
recordings across seven regional accent groups. SeamlessM4T v2-large is compared
with Whisper large-v2 followed by either NLLB-200 distilled 600M or NLLB-200
3.3B, using identical clips and references without fine-tuning. Corpus metrics
describe overall quality, while paired sentence-level chrF++ differences,
measuring character and word overlap, are assessed with speaker-clustered
bootstrap intervals and exploratory rank tests. Direct leads Cascade-600M by
7.00 points (95\% interval 6.45--7.60) and Cascade-3.3B by 4.99 points
(4.39--5.70). The larger translation model improves the cascade by 2.01 points
(1.61--2.42). Direct leads within every accent, but differences in between-accent
spread remain uncertain. Higher recognition error is weakly associated with
lower cascade scores and larger Direct advantages. The quality ranking persists
after excluding visibly marked outputs and counting each speaker once. These
findings support the observed ranking of the evaluated systems, while leaving
relative accent robustness unresolved. Differences in sentence content, speaker
composition, model family and training data prevent causal attribution to
accent or architecture.
\end{abstract}
