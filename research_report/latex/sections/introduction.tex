\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Speech-to-text translation converts spoken language into written text in another
language. For English-to-Chinese communication, a useful system should handle
speakers from different regional English backgrounds, rather than perform well
only when results are averaged across all speakers. English accent variation is
therefore relevant to evaluation. Studies of automatic speech recognition (ASR)
have reported performance disparities across English-language accents and
proposed methods for improving recognition of accents not represented during
training~\cite{dichristofano2022global,prabhu2023accentcodebooks}. However,
recognition quality and translation quality are different outcomes. More ASR
errors for an accent do not necessarily mean poorer translation quality.
Final translations must therefore be evaluated directly.

Two common approaches to speech translation expose different intermediate
information. A Cascaded speech translation (Cascaded ST) system first transcribes
the source audio using ASR and then applies machine translation (MT) to that
transcript. A Direct speech translation (Direct ST) system produces
target-language text from speech without an exposed source-language transcript.
Cascades allow separately developed ASR and MT components to be combined, but
their intermediate transcript can discard information or pass recognition
errors to MT. Direct systems avoid this explicit transcript interface, while
facing their own data and modelling challenges~\cite{sperber2020endtoend}.
Neither design guarantees better translation: comparisons can depend on the
systems, language direction and evaluation procedure~\cite{bentivogli2021cascade}.
Consequently, an overall score difference cannot alone establish which system
is less sensitive to regional accent variation.

This study addresses the following research question:
\begin{quote}
\emph{How does regional English accent affect the translation quality of
Direct and Cascaded speech-to-text translation systems?}
\end{quote}
Here, the question is investigated through observed associations on a frozen
evaluation set. Accent is not experimentally assigned, and the study does not
isolate it from sentence content, speaker characteristics or recording
conditions. In this study, accent robustness is defined through variation in mean sentence
chrF++ across the seven accent groups, including the difference between the
highest and lowest group means. Overall translation quality is reported
separately. A system can have higher scores in every group while still showing
uncertain evidence of a smaller between-accent spread.

This study compares three pretrained systems on the same 4,200 English recordings and
Simplified Chinese references. The Direct system uses SeamlessM4T v2-large.
The two cascades combine Whisper large-v2 with either NLLB-200 distilled 600M
or NLLB-200 3.3B. The recordings cover seven self-reported regional accent
groups, with 600 clips in each group. Speech and accent metadata originate from
Common Voice, and existing CoVoST 2 translations provide the target
references~\cite{ardila2020commonvoice,wang2021covost2}. These models are evaluated
without training, fine-tuning or accent-specific adaptation. The larger MT
variant allows an additional comparison within the cascade pipeline, rather
than treating one cascade configuration as representative of the whole approach.

The analysis tests whether paired mean sentence chrF++ differences are nonzero,
whether quality and system gaps vary across accent groups, and whether the
systems differ in their between-accent spread. It also examines the hypotheses
that higher normalized Whisper word error rate (WER) is associated with lower
cascade translation scores, and that the 3.3B cascade improves quality over the
600M cascade. Variation in that improvement across accents is examined
separately from its overall level. These comparisons organize the analysis;
they are not presented as a registered analysis plan. WER diagnoses the
cascade's ASR stage and is not used as a translation-quality measure for Direct
ST, which provides no intermediate transcript to score.

Corpus BLEU, chrF and chrF++ describe translation quality, while paired
statistics use sentence chrF++. Speaker-clustered resampling addresses the fact
that multiple clips can come from the same speaker. After the frozen runs,
additional analyses investigated literal \unk{} strings in Direct outputs and
examined decoding and subset sensitivity. These additional checks were conducted
after the main experiment. The original translations remained unchanged.

The study contributes a matched three-system benchmark across regional English
accent groups, an analysis that distinguishes system quality from accent
variation and statistical uncertainty, and diagnostic evidence about ASR
associations and Direct-output markers. Its findings concern the specified
checkpoints and evaluation set. Because model capacity, training data and
objectives also differ, the comparison cannot isolate the effect of system
architecture alone. The paper
first places this design in the relevant literature, then describes the
experimental procedure and reports quality, statistical, diagnostic and
sensitivity results before discussing their limitations.
