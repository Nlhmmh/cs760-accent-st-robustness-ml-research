\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:statistics}

The statistical analysis separates system quality, between-accent spread and
variation in paired system gaps. Speaker dependence determines the resampling
and primary testing units.

\subsubsection{Notation}
Let $i=1,\dots,N$ index clips ($N=4{,}200$), $a(i)\in\mathcal{A}$ the accent group ($|\mathcal{A}|=7$, $n_a=600$ clips each), $c(i)$ the pseudonymous speaker, and $s\in\mathcal{S}=\{\mbox{Direct},\mbox{Cascade-600M},\mbox{Cascade-3.3B}\}$ the system. Let $y_{is}$ be the sentence-level chrF++ of system $s$ on clip $i$. The accent mean and overall mean are
\begin{equation}
\bar y_{a,s}=\frac{1}{n_a}\sum_{i:\,a(i)=a} y_{is},\qquad
\bar y_{s}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} y_{is}.
\end{equation}
For two systems $s,s'$, the paired gap is $d_{is,s'}=y_{is}-y_{is'}$, with overall estimand $\Delta_{s,s'}=\bar y_s-\bar y_{s'}$ and within-accent estimand $\Delta_{a;s,s'}=\bar y_{a,s}-\bar y_{a,s'}$. A positive gap favours the first-named system. Robustness is operationalized as the between-accent spread of each system and the difference in spread between two systems:
\begin{equation}
R_s=\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\bar y_{a,s}-\min_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\bar y_{a,s},\qquad
\Delta R_{s,s'}=R_s-R_{s'}.
\end{equation}
All point estimates are clip-weighted.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty}
The 1,586 speakers contribute up to 88 clips each, so clips are not independent. Intervals therefore use a paired bootstrap~\cite{koehn2004significance} that resamples whole speakers as blocks, because an ordinary bootstrap understates uncertainty when utterances are correlated within speakers~\cite{liu2019blockwise}: within each accent, speakers are resampled with replacement and all of their clips are retained; the same resampled speakers are used for all three systems so that between-system differences stay paired. The procedure uses $B=1{,}000$ resamples with seed 760 and reports 95\% percentile intervals (2.5th and 97.5th percentiles) for $\Delta_{s,s'}$, $\Delta_{a;s,s'}$, $R_s$ and $\Delta R_{s,s'}$. As a sensitivity check, the same procedure treats each clip as its own cluster.

\subsubsection{Tests}
The analysis uses rank-based tests, which assume no particular score distribution,
as advised for NLP metrics whose distribution is unknown~\cite{dror2018}.
To ask whether a gap varies by accent, a Kruskal--Wallis
test~\cite{kruskal1952} is applied to $d_{is,s'}$ across the seven accents,
with $\epsilon^2=H/(n-1)$ as effect size ($n$ is the number of observations
in the test). Dunn pairwise tests~\cite{dunn1964} follow, with Holm
correction~\cite{holm1979} over the 21 accent pairs (per comparison and
analysis level). Cliff's $\delta=\Pr(x>x')-\Pr(x<x')$, for scores $x,x'$
drawn from the two groups, gives the pairwise effect size.

Within each accent, a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test assesses whether the
gap differs from zero, reported with the matched-pairs rank-biserial
correlation. Clip-level tests treat repeated clips from one speaker as
independent, so every rank test is repeated on speaker-mean scores and gaps
(one value per speaker; $n=1{,}586$), which is treated as the primary analysis.
Kruskal--Wallis is also applied to $y_{is}$ itself to test accent differences
within a system. Agreement between systems' accent orderings is summarized by
Spearman's $\rho$ over the seven accent means (no interval is computed).

\subsubsection{WER associations}
Associations between normalized Whisper WER and $y_{is}$ for each cascade, or WER and the Direct-minus-Cascade gap, are Spearman correlations over clips, with speaker-clustered bootstrap intervals (same settings as above).

\subsubsection{Scope}
Only the Dunn comparisons are multiplicity-corrected; all other $p$-values and the per-accent intervals are unadjusted, and the tests are exploratory. Repeated sentence content is not modelled jointly with speakers. A non-significant result is not treated as evidence of equality, since underpowered comparisons, such as those for accents with few speakers, support no firm conclusion in either direction~\cite{card2020power}. All results are associations rather than causal effects, because the systems differ in model family, size and training data.
