\subsection{Direct-Output Marker Diagnostics}
\label{sec:earlier-diagnostic-results}

\subsubsection{Marker frequency and decoding checks}
The frozen Direct run contains literal \unk{} in 508 of 4,200 outputs
(12.10\%), with 599 occurrences. Rates range from 10.33\% to 13.50\% across
the seven groups; these descriptive counts do not establish an accent effect.
Neither cascade contains this literal string.

In the 35-clip sensitivity
sample, the CPU FP32 greedy baseline exactly reproduces 33 archived
translations (94.29\%). Environment/precision differences therefore remain a
limitation of comparison with the original GPU run.

The visible marker is spelled by ordinary token sequences
\texttt{[249371, 2105, 248948]} or \texttt{[9614, 2105, 248948]} in this sample,
rather than special UNK ID~1. Suppressing ID~1 changes neither the greedy nor
beam-search outputs; no special UNK IDs occur in these conditions. Five-beam
search leaves the literal marker in 34 of 35 clips, compared with 35 of 35 under
greedy decoding. This sample-specific observation does not imply that special
UNK IDs are absent from other clips or from later diagnostic samples.

Blocking the two ordinary-token sequences removes the exact spelling from all
35 outputs, but all 35 instead contain \mbox{\texttt{<unk >}}. Corpus BLEU on this
same subset falls from 28.19 to 15.52, chrF from 24.85 to 19.15, and chrF++ from
18.86 to 14.49. Removing an exact-string flag therefore does not demonstrate
repair of the output. The remaining diagnostics examine whether marker
occurrence is associated with accent, input modality or source wording.
