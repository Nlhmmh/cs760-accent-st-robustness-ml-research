\label{sec:quality-results}

Direct scores highest on all four measures in Table~\ref{tab:overall},
and the ranking is the same on each: Direct, then Cascade-3.3B, then
Cascade-600M. The two cascades use identical saved Whisper transcripts, so
the difference between them reflects the change between the
two evaluated NLLB checkpoints at the translation stage. Moving from 600M to 3.3B adds 2.81 BLEU, 2.14 chrF and
1.62 chrF++. Direct's corpus lead shrinks as a result, from 9.75 to 6.94
BLEU (about 29\%), but it remains ahead on every metric. Whisper's
normalized WER is 5.97\% pooled (6.32\% as a per-clip mean) and is
identical for both cascades.

Direct's lead holds in every accent group (Table~\ref{tab:accent}). In
corpus terms it is ahead of Cascade-3.3B by 5.22 to 9.45 BLEU and 4.17 to
6.77 chrF++, and ahead of Cascade-600M by 7.69 to 11.46 BLEU. Cascade-3.3B
also scores higher than Cascade-600M in all seven groups, with
sentence-level gains from 1.17 to 3.25 chrF++, although
Section~\ref{sec:statistical-results} examines uncertainty in these gains.

The systems do not order the accents in the same way. Filipino is the
highest group for Direct and for Cascade-3.3B, but England is highest for
Cascade-600M. The lowest group is Southern African for Direct, India and
South Asia for Cascade-600M, and Hong Kong for Cascade-3.3B. Hong Kong is
therefore Direct's second-highest group and Cascade-3.3B's lowest. Across
groups, mean sentence chrF++ spans about 1.7 points for Direct, 2.70 for
Cascade-600M and 3.17 for Cascade-3.3B. Mean per-clip normalized WER by
group ranges from 4.29\% (United States) to 8.36\% (India and South Asia).
The three groups with the highest WER (India and South Asia, Malaysian and Hong Kong)
are also the three lowest-scoring groups for both cascades. Section~\ref{sec:statistical-results}
tests whether these differences are larger than sampling variation.

The descriptive ranking is therefore consistent across accent groups. The next
analysis examines uncertainty in the system gaps and asks whether differences
in between-accent spread support a claim of stronger accent robustness.
