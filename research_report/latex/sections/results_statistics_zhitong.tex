\subsubsection{System gaps}
Direct scores higher than both cascades overall, and Cascade-3.3B improves on Cascade-600M by about two points (Table~\ref{tab:paired-gaps}). Within every accent the Direct-minus-Cascade intervals exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:accent-gaps}), and the speaker-level matched-pairs rank-biserial correlations are positive throughout (0.53--0.93 against Cascade-600M; 0.39--0.91 against Cascade-3.3B). The Cascade-3.3B lift is positive in all seven accents but marginal for England English (0.18 to 2.19) and Hong Kong English (0.09 to 2.52), where speaker-level Wilcoxon tests give $p=0.138$ and $0.089$.

\subsubsection{Accent means and spread}
Accent moves each system far less than system choice does. The between-accent spread $R_s$ is 1.7 [1.4, 5.1] points for Direct, 2.7 [2.0, 4.7] for Cascade-600M and 3.2 [2.0, 5.7] for Cascade-3.3B; these intervals are right-skewed because a maximum minus a minimum of noisy means is biased upward. All three differences in spread have intervals that include zero (e.g.\ $-1.0$ [$-2.3$, 2.3] for Direct $-$ Cascade-600M), so the results do not establish that accent moves one system more than another. A speaker-level Kruskal--Wallis test detects accent differences only for Cascade-600M ($p=0.041$, $\epsilon^2=0.008$; Direct $p=0.185$, Cascade-3.3B $p=0.168$), a single uncorrected result. Accent orderings differ: the cascades largely agree (Spearman $\rho=0.82$), whereas Direct follows neither ($\rho=-0.18$ and $-0.07$; seven means only). Hong Kong English ranks second for Direct but sixth or seventh for the cascades, and its intervals are wide because it has only 41 speakers.

\subsubsection{Does the gap depend on accent}
\label{sec:statistical-accent-gap-variation}
Yes, but weakly. The Direct-minus-Cascade gap varies across accents at speaker level against Cascade-600M ($H(6)=21.4$, $p=0.0015$, $\epsilon^2=0.014$) and Cascade-3.3B ($H(6)=17.2$, $p=0.009$, $\epsilon^2=0.011$); clip-level tests agree. Against Cascade-600M the gap is largest for Hong Kong English (8.4) and smallest for England English (5.4); against Cascade-3.3B it is largest for Hong Kong English (7.0) and smallest for Southern African speakers (3.5). After Holm correction only 2 of 21 accent pairs differ against Cascade-600M and 1 of 21 against Cascade-3.3B, all with small effects (Cliff's $|\delta|\le 0.30$); Malaysian versus United States English is the pair common to both. Clip-level tests flag different pairs, so pair-level conclusions are fragile. Direct's flatter accent means suggest that the variation comes mostly from the cascades, but this is only suggestive given the uncertain spread differences. The Cascade-3.3B lift shows no detectable variation across accents ($p=0.168$ speaker level), which does not show that the lift is equal.

\subsubsection{ASR associations}
\label{sec:statistical-asr-associations}
Higher normalized WER is associated with lower cascade scores ($\rho=-0.19$ [$-0.22$, $-0.16$] for Cascade-600M; $-0.20$ [$-0.23$, $-0.17$] for Cascade-3.3B) and with a larger Direct advantage ($\rho=0.13$ [0.10, 0.16] and $0.17$ [0.14, 0.20]). All intervals exclude zero, overall and within each accent, but the associations are weak and observational, so they do not identify ASR error as the cause.

\subsubsection{Summary against the hypotheses}
The system gaps are nonzero overall and within every accent, Cascade-3.3B improves on Cascade-600M, and gap size varies by accent with small effects. Evidence that the systems differ in between-accent spread is lacking, and the WER associations are weak but in the expected direction. Direct's higher quality in every accent therefore does not establish stronger accent robustness. The per-accent intervals are unadjusted, Hong Kong English rests on 41 speakers, and the systems differ in model family, size and training data, so none of these findings can be attributed to accent handling or architecture alone.
