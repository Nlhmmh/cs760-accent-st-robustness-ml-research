clips, recognition-error subsets or repeated contributions from speakers.

\subsubsection{Marker-free subset}
The ranking is unchanged on the common subset of 3,692 unmarked clips.
Direct's corpus chrF++ rises from 25.08 to
26.32. Both cascades rise as well, Cascade-3.3B from 20.06 to 20.77 and
Cascade-600M from 18.44 to 19.10, so every system scores lower on the marked
clips. The paired gaps on the unmarked clips are +5.42 [4.75, 6.08] against
Cascade-3.3B and +7.50 [6.88, 8.11] against Cascade-600M, slightly above the
full-set values in Table~\ref{tab:paired-gaps}, and each per-accent
Direct-minus-Cascade interval stays above zero.

On the 508 marked clips alone Direct also scores above both cascades (corpus
chrF++ 17.55 against 15.46 and 14.22). The marked and unmarked groups contain
different sentences, so the 1.24-point rise in Direct's corpus chrF++ cannot
be separated from differences in sentence difficulty or interpreted as the
marker's effect.

\subsubsection{WER subsets and MT size}
The Direct advantage persists in both WER subsets, but its size differs.
Where normalized Whisper WER is zero, which covers 70.3\% of clips, Direct
leads Cascade-3.3B by +3.61 [3.04, 4.24]. Where normalized WER is positive,
the lead is +8.24 [7.38, 9.17]. These positive-WER clips make up 22.8\% of
United States English and 36.2\% of Malaysian English, the groups with the
smallest and one of the largest Direct-minus-Cascade-600M gaps in
Table~\ref{tab:accent-gaps}. The pattern is consistent with recognition errors
reaching the MT stage and with the correlations in
Section~\ref{sec:statistical-asr-associations}. However, the split is defined by
the cascade's own output, and the remaining gap at zero normalized WER cannot
be explained by counted transcription errors.

The larger MT checkpoint also improves scores in both subsets. Its lift is
+2.27 [1.76, 2.76] at zero normalized WER and +1.40 [0.90, 1.92] at positive
WER, giving a smaller observed gain where counted recognition errors occur.
The overall and per-accent comparison is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:statistical-results}; these subset results concern the two
evaluated NLLB checkpoints and do not predict the performance of other MT
models.

\subsubsection{Speaker dependence}
With one clip per speaker the gaps are +6.19 [5.37, 6.90] against Cascade-600M, +4.69 [3.94, 5.51] against Cascade-3.3B and +1.50 [0.91, 2.16] between the cascades. These agree with the speaker-clustered estimates on the full set.

\subsubsection{Fallback}
Replacing Direct's output with the Cascade-3.3B translation on the 508 marked clips lowers corpus chrF++ from 25.08 to 24.89 and corpus BLEU from 38.86 to 38.54. On these clips the marked Direct outputs score above the cascade's translations, so the substitution did not improve scores.

\subsubsection{Lowest-scoring clips}
In five of the seven groups, at least half of Direct's ten lowest-scoring clips score exactly zero. The cascades' worst clips in India and South Asia and in Hong Kong English carry mean WER between 0.31 and 0.56, against 0.06 over the whole set. Direct's worst clips carry mean WER of about 0.1, and 14\% of them contain the marker, near the 12.1\% base rate. Several references are non-literal. The reference for "There you have it!" is \begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}给你！\end{CJK*}, and the literal \begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}这就是它!\end{CJK*} scores zero against it. A low automatic score on such a clip measures reference matching and identifies no system error.

Together, the subset and speaker checks support the stability of the observed
system ranking. Their implications for accent robustness and the unresolved
sources of the system gaps are considered in the Discussion.
